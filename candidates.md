# Candidates and tournament record — entry v2

The previous entry (v1, prose, 257 code points) stood at #129 of 2,916 on the
development board with judge 822.728, below the final-heat cut (top 80,
833.509). Recon of the board's top texts showed the winning DNA: verse with
short lines whose breaks slow the reading, a concrete bodily scene with a
family figure, and cosmic scale arriving *through* that small scene, landing
in stillness or sleep. Eight new candidates were written to that DNA — each
with a fresh scene of its own (no boat-and-coat, no bucket) — and played a
full round-robin tournament together with the v1 text as a ninth, comparison
text. Every game was judged twice, once in each order, with the competition's
published relaxation-judge brief applied in its own order:

1. it relaxes you → 2. the calm comes from the text itself, never from
instruction → 3. craft: rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading →
4. it dwarfs you → 5. freshness. The first criterion where a pair differs
decides, unless a later one differs by much more. Margins are the brief's
1 (by a hair) to 5 (overwhelming).

## The candidates

### C1 — Night ferry (268 code points)

    The ferry crosses in the dark.
    You sleep on the bench,
    your head on your folded coat.
    Below, the engine hums low.
    Water slaps the hull, slow.
    Your breath keeps time with it.
    The ferryman does not hurry.
    Behind you, the shore you left
    lets go of its lights, one by one.

### C2 — Rain on the tent (243 code points)

    Rain on the tent, all night.
    Your father sleeps beside you,
    one hand still on the rope.
    You count the drips on the canvas,
    each one finding its way down.
    Above the thin roof, the mountain
    stands in the dark rain,
    saying nothing, keeping watch.

### C3 — Father mending the clock (254 code points)

    Your father mends the clock.
    His hands are slow with the small screws.
    You hold the lamp for him.
    Tick. Tick. The room leans closer.
    When its heart starts again,
    it beats soft and unhurried,
    and the evening goes on ticking,
    gentle, around the two of you.

### C4 — The old well (245 code points)

    Before the village wakes,
    you draw water from the old well.
    The rope creaks in the cold.
    The pail comes up slow and dark.
    Hands long turned to dust dug this,
    deeper than the tower is tall —
    and still the water rises,
    patient, to meet your hands.

### C5 — Night train (247 code points)

    The night train rocks you.
    Across from you, your grandmother sleeps,
    her head against the glass.
    Fields go by without names.
    The moon keeps pace with the train —
    behind the trees, then clear again —
    in no hurry to arrive,
    asking nothing of anyone.

### C6 — Porch swing (241 code points) — WINNER

    On the porch swing,
    your grandmother shells peas,
    the bowl between her knees.
    Pod, peas, hush. Pod, peas, hush.
    The chain creaks, slow.
    Evening comes down over the fields
    as it has ten thousand times,
    and will, long after the swing is still.

### C7 — Paper boat (255 code points)

    You fold a boat from old newspaper
    and set it on the evening stream.
    It goes on without you —
    past the reeds, past the bridge,
    smaller and smaller, still upright.
    The stream hands it to the river,
    the river to the dark sea.
    It sails on for you, all night.

### C8 — Mother braiding hair (260 code points)

    Your mother braids your hair
    by the last of the light.
    Her fingers are slow,
    section over section.
    Now and then she hums,
    almost under her breath.
    Beyond the glass, the stars
    are opening, one by one,
    too many ever to count —
    and her hands keep their slow time.

### V1 — The previous entry's text (257 code points, comparison only)

    Some of the light on your window tonight left its star before there were
    words for light. It crossed all that dark without hurrying, and it
    arrives now, quietly: on the sill, on the floor, on the back of your
    hand. It came so far to rest somewhere so small.

## Why the winner beats v1 convincingly

C6 beat V1 in both orders, by margins 3 and 2 — the widest margins any
candidate scored against it. Criteria 1 and 2 are level: both texts settle
the reader and neither instructs. The first difference is criterion 3, and
it is clear, not a hair: v1 is one prose paragraph whose long clauses carry
the reader along at sentence speed, while C6's short verse lines, the
creaking-chain pauses and the spoken refrain "Pod, peas, hush. Pod, peas,
hush." physically slow the reading down. Criterion 4 does not overturn it:
v1's ancient light is grand, but C6's close — the evening that has come down
ten thousand times and will come down still, long after the swing is still —
is an image of the same growing kind, rooted in the body of the scene
instead of floating free of it. Three other candidates (C8, C4, C5) also
beat V1 in both orders; V1 finished sixth of nine.
